# Learn from a supplied graphic

Identify the visible principle before suggesting an adaptation. Use only patterns actually supported by the inspected reference.

| Principle | What to inspect | Original adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Recognizable situation | A specific familiar problem or moment | Show a relevant situation for our audience |
| Demonstration | An actual product or process detail | Use our own approved screenshot or demonstration |
| Contrast | Two visibly different states or choices | Show a truthful contrast without invented results |
| Useful discovery | A detail that makes a worthwhile lesson clear | Reveal our own source-backed detail |
| Objection | A real concern the graphic answers | Address a supported concern in our own words |
| Subject and composition | Focal point, scale, relationships and visual tension | Use original subjects and composition to serve our message |

Example: a reference makes a small overlooked detail the focal point. Explain that observed principle, then suggest highlighting a relevant detail from our business with original artwork and wording. Do not reproduce the reference's distinctive design or assume its engagement transfers.

Offer only as many adaptations as useful or requested. This is asset/reference analysis, not the framework's ten-concept or ten-hook development stage.
